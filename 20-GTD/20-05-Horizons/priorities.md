# Current Priorities & Focus Areas

Projects declare which objective they serve via an `objective:` frontmatter field whose value links to a heading below. The weekly review's objective scorecard and the gtd-coach audit both check this linkage — when objectives change, update the headings here and the checks follow automatically.

**O1 is the big bet.** The yearly review's central directive was to pick one large strategic project and execute it end-to-end rather than accumulating side projects. The model permitting-timeline bill is that project. When O1 competes with anything below it, O1 wins.

## O1: Get a model state permitting-timeline bill introduced in two legislatures
- KR1: model bill text finalized and reviewed by two outside counsel
- KR2: sponsors secured in two states
- KR3: one explainer published that legislators' staff actually cite

## O2: Build expertise in transmission siting and interconnection policy
- KR1: read and catalogue the twenty foundational sources in `40-PKM`
- KR2: publish one analysis that a practitioner would consider correct

## O3: Improve the substance of federal housing-supply legislation
- KR1: testimony or written comments delivered to at least one committee
- KR2: two concrete amendments proposed to staff, with text

## O4: Build a strategic network of congressional and state staff
- KR1: two or three relationships with senior staff deep enough to move policy
- KR2: a standing short memo of current takes, sent to the people who should see it

## O5: Grow public profile and communication skills
- KR1: publish short-form content weekly
- KR2: one long-form piece per quarter

## O6: Contribute to organization-wide initiatives and mentor junior staff
- KR1: mentor one research assistant through a full project
- KR2: run one internal workshop

## Working Better
- One big project at a time. Supporting roles are fine; a second big project is not.
- Every project has an unblocked next action or it is not a project.
- Weekly review is non-negotiable. Skipping it skips the backup too.
